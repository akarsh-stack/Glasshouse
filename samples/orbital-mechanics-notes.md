# Kessler Station — Operations Handbook (Excerpt)

## Station vitals

Kessler Station maintains a nominal orbital altitude of 412 kilometres with an
inclination of 51.6 degrees. The station completes one orbit every 92.7 minutes,
which works out to 15.5 orbits per day. Total pressurised volume is 916 cubic
metres across eleven modules.

## Power budget

The four solar array wings generate a combined 84 kilowatts at beginning of life,
degrading approximately 2.1 percent per year. Battery storage is 240 ampere-hours
across six lithium-ion units, sized to carry the station through the 35-minute
eclipse portion of each orbit with a 40 percent depth-of-discharge margin.

Peak load during a spacewalk is 71 kilowatts, which is why EVA operations are
scheduled to begin at orbital noon rather than during eclipse.

## Attitude control and reboost

Attitude is held by four control moment gyroscopes, each storing 4,700 newton-metre
seconds of angular momentum. When two or more gyroscopes fail, the station reverts
to thruster-based control, which consumes roughly 1.4 kilograms of propellant per
hour and is therefore treated as a contingency mode only.

Atmospheric drag lowers the orbit by about 90 metres per day at solar minimum and
up to 400 metres per day at solar maximum. Reboost burns are performed roughly
every six weeks, raising the orbit by 3 to 8 kilometres per burn.

## Thermal control

Waste heat is rejected through two ammonia loops feeding six radiator panels with a
combined capacity of 70 kilowatts. The internal water loop is held between 4 and 17
degrees Celsius. A single-loop failure caps the station's power draw at 38 kilowatts
until the loop is restored, which means science payloads are shed first.

## Life support

The oxygen generation assembly electrolyses water at 2.3 kilograms of oxygen per
day, sufficient for a crew of six with a 30 percent margin. Carbon dioxide is
scrubbed to below 4 millimetres of mercury partial pressure. Water recovery runs at
93 percent, so consumable resupply is dominated by food mass rather than water.

## Debris avoidance

Conjunction assessments are screened 72 hours out. A predicted miss distance inside
the 25-by-25-by-4 kilometre control box triggers a debris avoidance manoeuvre,
typically a 0.5 metre per second prograde burn executed 2 hours before closest
approach. The station performed nine such manoeuvres in the previous calendar year.
